Parked ideas that are **not** part of the current design and not approved; they carry no prerequisites for any task. Add an idea here instead of keeping it in a GDD.

| Squares that act once and then vanish (the earlier trap), as a prop, an “array” laid on a square, or another kind of thing | Reshape, second step (user, 2026-11-13: still likes the idea, does not yet know how to build it in; to be thought about later) | Surges and traps were removed and ground acts as long as it lies. Needs its own life rule next to ground (2 rounds) and props (HP); keep “no damage at range without a turn since the cast” in mind. **Decided by the user on 2026-11-15:** they exist as walkable props (a trap; [battle.md §4.7](../battle.md#47-props)); which traps and runes stay is content under T41. |

| Props or ground that damage neighbouring squares (on touch, or every N turns/rounds without being destroyed) | T38 discussion (parked by the user 2026-11-12) | Would be damage nobody cast with its own clock; touches “zones never deal damage” and the fixed, previewed prop aftermath. A larger hazard ground or a spiky prop (T38) covers most of the intent. **Partly built as a draft on 2026-11-14** (Ember Brazier at the round end, Thorn Idol on a hit); whether that reopens the parked idea is a decision under T41. |
| Ground that displaces (Ice: a monster pushed or pulled onto it slides 1 square further in the same direction if that square is free) | Surge discussion (parked by the user 2026-11-12) | Not now: the build just gained many displacement sources (every Strike, zones at cast and at turn end, movement with landing displacement, pushing Surges and traps, Gong, Vortex Urn, Storm Drum) and nobody has played them; landings must stay easy to foresee; ground is permanent and has two clear roles so far (hazard, blessed). Ice is the smallest candidate because it only extends an existing displacement and needs no direction per square. Revisit if play shows that displacement falls one square short too often. Not recommended: Current ground (pushes at turn end; overlaps the push zones, needs a direction per square) and spring ground (throws whoever enters it; makes voluntary paths hard to plan). |
